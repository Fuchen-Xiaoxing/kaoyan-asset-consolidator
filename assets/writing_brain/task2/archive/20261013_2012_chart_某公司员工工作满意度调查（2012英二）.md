## 终版高分范文

As is distinctly illustrated in the table, a company's staff differ sharply in job satisfaction by age. Those aged 41 to 50 are the most dissatisfied, with 64% unhappy and none satisfied. Those over 50 remain the most content, while the young under 40 stay uncertain.

This gap results from their different expectations and pressures. To begin with, employees aged 41 to 50 serve as the backbone of the company, yet their effort is rarely matched by better pay or promotions. Meanwhile, they must shoulder the burden of ageing parents and growing children, so their expectations far exceed their rewards. In sharp contrast, young workers carry lighter burdens and still have years to navigate their career paths, which leaves half of them undecided. Finally, workers over 50 are nearing retirement, so they value stability more than ever and have consciously lowered their goals.

To bridge this gap, companies ought to open up promotion channels and provide targeted training for middle-aged staff. Besides, employees should update their skills and adjust their expectations. Only in this way can both sides benefit.
