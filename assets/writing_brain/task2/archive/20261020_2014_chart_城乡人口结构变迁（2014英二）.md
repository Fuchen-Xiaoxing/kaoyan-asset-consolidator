## 终版高分范文

As is distinctly illustrated in the chart, the urban population surged dramatically from 300 million in 1990 to 458 million in 2000, and then to 666 million in 2010, while the rural population fell steadily from 834 million to 674 million, leaving the two figures almost equal.

This dramatic shift stems largely from China's rapid economic development. First, rapid industrialisation has fuelled the rise of countless factories and industrial parks, which generate abundant job opportunities. More importantly, the gap between urban and rural areas keeps widening. Higher wages and superior schools and hospitals draw millions of rural residents to cities, whereas farming alone can barely support a family. As a result, cities keep expanding into the surrounding countryside, and many villages are left with only the elderly and children.

This trend, a microcosm of China's urbanisation, indicates that cities offer better pay and services, while the countryside lags far behind. The government should therefore step up investment in rural areas and upgrade rural industries, so as to meet the people's ever-growing needs for a better life. Only in this way can the countryside retain its people.
