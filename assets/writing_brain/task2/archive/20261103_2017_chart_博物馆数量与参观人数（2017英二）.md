## 终版高分范文

As is distinctly illustrated in the chart, the number of museums in China rose steadily from 4,165 in 2013 to 4,692 two years later, whereas the figure for visitors surged from 637.8 million to 781.1 million, nearly twice the pace of the former.

This gap stems mainly from two factors. The museum network has largely taken shape, which naturally slows the expansion of new venues. After all, a single museum requires huge investment and years of construction. In sharp contrast, the number of visitors has grown far faster. The public now shows a keener interest in Chinese history and cultural relics, largely because urbanisation and industrial upgrading have brought people more leisure time and higher incomes, enabling them to travel and learn about historical sites. Meanwhile, the Internet has made museums far more visible, drawing visitors from across the country.

This trend is a microcosm of the improvement in Chinese people's quality of life, yet it also exposes a supply-demand imbalance. The government should therefore step up investment in museums and upgrade their services, so that supply can keep pace with people's ever-growing needs.
